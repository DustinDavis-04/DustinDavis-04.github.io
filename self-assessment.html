Professional Self Assessment

Growth Through Computer Science

This assessment reflects on the skills I developed throughout the Computer Science program, how those skills have influenced my professional growth, and how the work included in this portfolio represents that development.

Development

Building a Stronger Approach to Technology

One of the biggest ways I changed throughout my Computer Science program was my overall approach to technology and programming. When I first started, I was apprehensive about learning multiple programming languages because I thought I would need to remember all of the syntax and specific details for each one. As I progressed through the program, I realized that while syntax is important, being a successful programmer is not about memorizing every command. There are plenty of resources available that can help verify syntax when needed. What became more important to me was understanding the foundation behind programming and learning the correct way to approach a problem. Concepts such as object oriented programming helped me understand how to organize code, separate responsibilities, and create solutions that are easier to understand and maintain.

That change in perspective also made me much more confident when approaching new programming languages, tools and techniques. Once I understood that many programming concepts carry over between languages, learning something new did not feel as overwhelming. Instead of feeling like I had to start over each time, I could focus on the features or techniques that were unique to that language or environment. This helped me become more comfortable with the idea that I can continue learning new technologies throughout my career instead of feeling limited to the ones I already know.

Professional Growth

Connecting Software and Systems Administration

My professional goals also changed while I progressed through the program. When I started, I was mainly interested in pursuing a career in programming, and I was especially interested in eventually creating my own video games. That is still something I would like to pursue on my own, but my immediate career goals have shifted after moving into a Systems Administrator role. I now see systems administration and programming as skills that can work together instead of being separate. My programming background gives me another way to approach problems, automate tasks, understand how applications interact with infrastructure, and continue expanding what I can do in a technical environment.

The program also strengthened my attention to detail and the way I approach problem solving. I already had a strong foundation in attention to detail from my military experience, but programming took that to another level. Small mistakes in syntax, logic, or the way classes and objects work together can affect an entire application. This taught me to be more deliberate about checking my work and thinking about how a change in one part of a system can affect another. I also learned the importance of building and testing software in smaller increments. Testing each section as I build it makes it easier to find where a problem started and reduces the amount of code I have to go back through when something stops working. This reinforced an approach I already used when troubleshooting, starting with the simplest possible cause and gradually moving toward more advanced solutions.

Communication and Collaboration

Working With Different Audiences

Another area where I have developed professionally is communicating technical information to people with different levels of technical experience. In one situation, I helped a user whose Microsoft Authenticator connection stopped working after he received a new phone. The normal troubleshooting is to remove and then re-add the user's account, but the user was not familiar with many of the terms or actions I initially started with. I changed my approach by asking him to describe what he saw on each screen, giving him one action at a time, and then adjusting my instructions based on what he saw next. We were eventually able to restore his access. That experience reinforced for me that technical knowledge is only part of solving a problem. Being patient, changing the way I explain something based on the person I am helping, and making sure they do not feel uncomfortable for asking questions are just as important.

I have also developed my ability to work through technical problems with other people. During an email outage in my work environment, I worked with my team to review Exchange Online mail flow and narrow the problem down to Proofpoint. After identifying the issue, we put a temporary workaround in place and divided the remaining work, including checking MX records and confirming that external mail flow continued to work. That experience reinforced the importance of sharing findings, dividing responsibilities, and working together to solve a larger technical problem.

Computer Science Foundation

Algorithms, Software Engineering, and Databases

My coursework also helped me understand that a working solution is not automatically the best solution. Through data structures and algorithms, I learned to consider how data is organized, how an algorithm processes that data, and how design choices can affect efficiency as the amount of data increases. Concepts such as time complexity and Big O notation gave me a way to compare possible solutions instead of only looking at whether they produced the correct result. I applied this more directly during my capstone when working with the Grazioso Salvare project, where matching intake and outcome records and ranking animals required me to think about efficiency, organization, and the tradeoffs involved in the approach I selected.

Software engineering and database development also became much easier to understand once I stopped looking at an application as one large piece of code. Throughout the program, I worked with web applications, Python, Android development, relational and non-relational databases, and different development environments. I learned how interfaces, application logic, classes, databases, and other components work together while still having their own responsibilities. Database coursework also gave me a better understanding of how the way data is structured affects the application using it. During the capstone, I was able to apply this by improving the structure of the Mobile Inventory App database and separating information such as manufacturers, categories, and locations into a more organized relational design.

Security Mindset

Designing With Security in Mind

Security also became a more important part of how I think about technology. Earlier in the program, I was more likely to think about whether an application worked correctly before thinking about how it could be misused. Secure coding coursework helped change that approach by making me consider input validation, access to data, vulnerabilities, and what an attacker could potentially do with an application. That mindset applies beyond programming and is also important in systems administration, where permissions, authentication, configuration and protecting organizational resources are part of everyday decisions. I now try to think about security while a solution is being designed rather than treating it as something that can simply be added afterward.

Project Archive

Bringing the Artifacts Together

The three artifacts included in my ePortfolio show different parts of the skills I developed throughout the program. Travlr Getaways demonstrates software design and engineering through a full stack web application and the improvements I made to its functionality, validation, and user experience. Grazioso Salvare demonstrates algorithms and data structures through data preparation, intake and outcome matching, rescue suitability scoring, and ranking. The Mobile Inventory App demonstrates database development through an Android application and improvements to the way inventory information is organized and stored. Together, the three projects show that I can work across different languages, platforms, development environments, and types of problems instead of being limited to one area of computer science.

Completing the Computer Science program has given me more than experience with individual programming languages. It has given me a stronger process for learning new technology, breaking down problems, testing solutions, communicating with different audiences, working with other technical professionals, and considering security and maintainability while designing a solution. Those are the skills I believe will be the most useful as I continue developing as a Systems Administrator and continue using software development as another part of my technical skill set. The projects that follow provide examples of how I have applied those skills and how my abilities have developed throughout the program.
